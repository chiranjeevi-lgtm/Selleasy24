===============================================================================
CROSS-CUTTING DESIGN PRINCIPLES
===============================================================================

These are product principles, not phase items. They govern every feature we
ship from Phase 0 onwards. Each principle is a direct anti-pattern to
Square Yards' most consistent third-party complaint themes (per Trustpilot /
MouthShut / Glassdoor aggregation, see squareyards-reference.md §12).

1. Written-commitment discipline
   Every discount, cashback, refund, callback, or service promise is captured
   as a signed PDF in-app before it takes effect. Verbal promises are
   invalid by design. Square Yards' #1 complaint is verbal promises
   (₹50k-₹1L cashbacks) that never convert to writing.

2. Escrow-milestone for services
   Any premium tier, featured listing, or service upsell above ₹5,000 flows
   through milestone-based escrow (RazorpayX or bank escrow). Direct
   anti-pattern to the Interior Company failure mode: 60% completion at
   90% payment, ~₹12L customer losses documented.

3. Hard cap on outbound contact frequency
   After a buyer submits an enquiry: max 2 platform-initiated calls in
   72 hours, max 1 SMS/day for 7 days, then silence. Anti-pattern to
   pre-booking hyper-chase complaints. Configurable, but the default is
   restrained.

4. Verification recency as public signal
   Every listing card renders "verified N hours ago by officer #V-023"
   next to the badge. Turns our active pre-publish verification into a
   visible superiority signal over Square Yards' passive-badge model
   (their badge has no timestamp, no officer identity).

5. On-domain trust content
   Reviews, verification records, fraud reports, complaint outcomes all
   live on our own domain, not routed to Google/Trustpilot/MouthShut.
   Keeps the trust conversation on our surface (SEO + reputation win).

6. Explainable AI only
   Investment scores, valuations, recommendations always show inputs,
   confidence, and last-computed timestamp. Never a black-box score.
   Legal exposure under RBI unregulated-advice guidelines is real; a
   transparent formula is the safe path.

PHASE 1.5 — SEO TEMPLATING LAYER (2-3 WEEKS)   [NEW — was missing]
===============================================================================

This phase was NOT in the original roadmap. It is the single biggest
missing piece per the Square Yards audit. Ranks #1, #2, #3 in the
replication priority list — collectively the highest value-per-effort in
the entire program.

Square Yards' SEO footprint is built on THREE URL templates, each producing
thousands of pages. We replicate the templates for Hyderabad only —
~2,000 SEO pages generated from templates in one sprint.

FAMILY A — CANNED FILTER PERMALINKS (rank #1)
  Pattern:
    /sale/{bhk}-bhk-{propertyType}-in-{locality}-hyderabad
    /sale/{bhk}-bhk-{propertyType}-in-{locality}-hyderabad-between-{lo}-{hi}
    /rent/{bhk}-bhk-{propertyType}-in-{locality}-hyderabad
    /sale/{propertyType}-for-sale-in-{locality}-hyderabad
  Content: same search results as the equivalent /?filter=… URL, but
    canonical, with a real H1 like
    "3 BHK Flats for Sale in Kondapur, Hyderabad between ₹75 Lac and ₹1 Cr"
  Generation: precompute ~2,000 slugs from top 20 localities × property
    types × BHK × 5 budget bands. Render as static or ISR with 6-hour
    revalidation.

FAMILY B — LOCALITY OVERVIEW PAGES (rank #2)
  Pattern: /localities/{slug}  (e.g. /localities/kondapur-hyderabad)
  Sections:
    - Hero: locality name + one-line price rate
    - Map (Leaflet, reuses Phase 1 code)
    - Price trend chart (1yr / 3yr / 5yr appreciation — depends on Phase 2)
    - Pros / cons (from locality reviews UGC — Phase 2)
    - Connectivity (schools, hospitals, malls, transit — from OSM data)
    - Top projects in this locality
    - Live resale + rent inventory (embeds existing search)
    - Reviews (UGC — Phase 2)
    - FAQ (Q&A per locality, hand-seeded to start)
    - Nearby localities
  Data: Locality + LocalitySnapshot (Phase 2) + Listing + Project
  Note: page structure ships in Phase 1.5 with placeholder sections;
    price-trend + reviews sections populate as Phase 2 lands.

FAMILY C — PRICE-RATE MICRO-PAGES (rank #3)
  Pattern: /property-rates/{locality}-hyderabad
  Content: ONE H1, ONE number, ONE delta
    "Kondapur Property Rates — ₹10,350/sq ft, +8.3% (3yr) | Aug 2026"
    Plus short breakdown table (avg by BHK, avg by property type)
  Generation: static, revalidated daily

SEO INFRASTRUCTURE
  - sitemap.xml auto-generated from all three families + core pages
  - robots.txt hardened (explicit allow for the three families)
  - Structured data (JSON-LD) on every page:
      RealEstateListing on listing detail
      Place on locality overview
      LocalBusiness on homepage
      FAQPage on locality overview
  - Open Graph + Twitter cards on all generated pages
  - Canonical URLs to prevent duplicate content penalties

Depends on: nothing outside existing data (though Phase 2 makes the
  locality pages meaningfully richer).
Leverages: Next.js 15 static generation + ISR, existing search API.

PHASE 2 — DATA INTELLIGENCE LAYER (8-12 WEEKS)
===============================================================================

Reframed based on Square Yards audit. Their Data Intelligence dashboard —
8.6M gov-registered transactions, ET Proptech Innovation of the Year 2026
award — is the single biggest competitive moat. We only need Hyderabad
district data to reach parity in the local market, a much smaller data

FEATURE 1 — TELANGANA REGISTRAR TRANSACTION INGESTION      [REFRAMED]
  Originally scoped as "price history from our own snapshots"; expanded to
  include actual government-registered sale transactions.
  Data source: Telangana Registration & Stamps Department
    (registration.telangana.gov.in) — Encumbrance Certificate + Sub-Registrar
    records. Public, published for legal purposes.
  Approach:
    - Option A: scrape district-wise EC data (grey area on ToS; get legal
      counsel review before commissioning)
    - Option B: partner with Karza / Signzy / Digio for structured feed
      (₹50-200 per query, clean legal path)
    - Recommended: Option B for launch, Option A as fallback
  New Prisma model: RegisteredTransaction
    Fields: survey number, address, sale price, sale date, buyer type,
            registration doc reference, source
  Surface:
    - "Last 10 registered transactions" widget on listing detail +
      locality overview pages
    - Actual-vs-listed price delta
      ("Listed 8% above last registered sale in this building")
    - Feeds e-valuation model (Feature 4)
  Why it matters: this is Square Yards' award-winning moat and their
    single biggest competitive advantage against 99acres / Housing.com /
    NoBroker. Ranked #5 in replication priority.

FEATURE 2 — LISTING PRICE HISTORY
  New Prisma model: ListingPriceSnapshot
    Fields: listingId, price, changedAt, changedBy
  Backfill: initial snapshot for every existing listing
  Hook: listing update endpoint inserts snapshot on price change
  UI: "Price dropped ₹15L in 30 days" callout on detail page
  Auto-alert saved-search subscribers on price movement (Phase 1 push
    prereq needed)

FEATURE 3 — RERA CROSS-CHECK AUTOMATION                    [DIFFERENTIATED]
  Nightly BullMQ job: sync-rera-tsrera
  New Prisma model: RegulatoryRegistration
    Fields: reraNumber, projectName, promoter, registeredAt, expiresAt,
            completionDeadline, status, lastCheckedAt
  Auto-flag listings with mismatched / expired / not-found RERA numbers
  Verifier UI shows auto-check result before opening the file
  KEY DIFFERENTIATOR (from Cross-Cutting Principle #4):
    Render "Verified 4 hours ago by officer #V-023 · RERA current" on
    every card. Square Yards' passive badge has NO timestamp, NO officer
    identity. This is a defensible marketing claim they cannot match
    without breaking their own SLA.

FEATURE 4 — LOCALITY ANALYTICS (feeds Phase 1.5 pages)
  New Prisma model: LocalitySnapshot (daily row per locality)
    Fields: localityId, date, medianPricePerSqft, listingCount,
            avgDaysOnMarket, yoy1yr, yoy3yr, yoy5yr, transactionCount
  Nightly BullMQ job: compute-locality-analytics
  Consumed by: Phase 1.5 locality overview pages, listing benchmark widget,
    investment score

FEATURE 5 — AUTOMATED E-VALUATIONS
  Backend service: ValuationService
  Comparables model: PostGIS radius + configuration match + area
    adjustment + recency weighting (uses Feature 2 + Feature 1 data)
  New API endpoint: POST /valuations/estimate
  UI:
    - Standalone tool page: /tools/valuation
    - Auto-shown market comparison on every listing detail
    - Owner-facing tool ("What should I list at?") in seller dashboard
  Anchors to Cross-Cutting Principle #6: always shows inputs, confidence,
    last-computed timestamp.

FEATURE 6 — INVESTMENT SCORE v1 (transparent formula)
  Weighted signal:
    +25 RERA verified + current
    +20 locality YoY appreciation (scaled)
    +15 developer track record (from Builder profile — Phase 1)
    +15 infrastructure proximity (metro, ORR, airport — PostGIS distances)
    +10 amenities completeness
    +10 price vs. locality median
    +5  photo/document completeness
  Column: Listing.investmentScore, Project.investmentScore
  UI: badge on cards ("Score 82 · Strong") + explanation panel on detail
  Explanation panel shows every input + weight (Cross-Cutting Principle #6)

FEATURE 7 — LOCALITY REVIEWS UGC        [NEW]
  Square Yards /reviews/locality/*, ranks #10 in replication priority
  Route: /localities/{slug}/reviews (linked from overview page)
  New Prisma model: LocalityReview
    Fields: userId, localityId, rating (1-5), pros, cons,
            tenure (years at locality), moderationStatus
  Moderation: routes through existing MODERATOR role
  Anti-spam: verified account + one review per user per locality
  Surface: on Phase 1.5 locality overview pages (pros/cons section)

FEATURE 8 — EMI + ELIGIBILITY CALCULATOR       [MOVED FROM PHASE 5]
  Square Yards /emi-eligibility-calculator, ranks #7
  Standalone tool page: /tools/emi-calculator
  Client-side only (no backend needed at this stage)
  Inputs: loan amount, tenure, interest rate
  Outputs: EMI, total interest, amortisation schedule
  Second tool: eligibility calculator (income, existing EMIs, age, tenure)
    → returns loan amount range
  Embed on: every project detail, every listing with price >₹50L
  Why moved from Phase 5: pure client-side, no bank partnerships needed;
    ships in one day; feeds lead generation for Phase 5 when it lands.

FEATURE 9 — ANTI-FRAUD HELP DESK (extended from Phase 1)
  Already in Phase 1 as basic page. Extend here:
  - Public directory of registered TSRERA agents (scraped nightly)
  - "Check if a broker/agent is registered" tool
  - Case-outcome log (moderated, PII-scrubbed, on-domain trust content)

Leverages: existing verification data, PostGIS, pgvector, BullMQ.

Hyderabad buyer intent.

THE HONEST CUT (updated post-audit)
===============================================================================

Square Yards has ₹2,086 Cr revenue and 8,000+ employees. Their biggest
consumer complaint is that their OFFLINE handoff breaks the trust their
software promises (Trustpilot / MouthShut aggregation shows a consistent
pattern: pre-booking hyper-chase, post-booking silence, verbal promises
never in writing, Interior Company delivery failures).

SellEasy24's wedge is precisely that gap: active pre-publish verification,
on-domain trust content, written-commitment discipline, escrow-milestone
service tiers, hard cap on outbound contact. None of those require Square
Yards' scale.

Ranked by defensibility for a small Hyderabad-focused team:

  1. Cross-Cutting Principles
     Product / design commitments — free to ship, hard for a big
     commission-driven org to copy.

  2. Phase 1.5 SEO templating
     Traffic acquisition — biggest lever we're missing today.

  3. Phase 2 registrar-data ingestion
     Data moat. Hyderabad-only is manageable; national is not.

  4. Verification recency signal (Cross-Cutting + Phase 2)
     "Verified 4h ago by officer #V-023" — Square Yards' passive badge
     cannot match this.

  5. Phase 3 on-site conversational search
     They left this gap open. We take it.

Phases 1.5, 2, and 3 alone give SellEasy24 a defensible product that
competes on more than "another listings site." Everything from Phase 4
onwards is scale-up territory.
